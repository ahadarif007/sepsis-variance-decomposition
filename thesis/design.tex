\chapter{System Design}
\label{ch:design}

This chapter describes how the pipeline is built: the architecture that turns
raw critical-care tables into a continuously updated, externally validated
sepsis-onset risk. The design links directly to the best practices established
in Chapter~\ref{ch:review}: leakage-safe temporal features, landmarking for
real-time risk, informative-missingness encoding, and a transportable
closed-form model.

\section{Overall Architecture}
The system is organised as a linear pipeline of numbered, single-purpose stages
grouped into three layers---\textbf{Extract}, \textbf{Model}, and
\textbf{Evaluate}---as shown in Figure~\ref{image:sysArchitecture}. Each stage
reads the artefact written by the previous stage and writes its own, so any
stage can be re-run in isolation. A single configuration module
(\texttt{config.py}) is the sole source of truth for file paths, clinical item
identifiers, antibiotic name lists, and Sepsis-3 thresholds.

\section{Extract Layer (Scripts 01--08)}

The extract layer builds the temporal substrate that the models consume. It
comprises five stages:

\paragraph{Stage 01: Exploratory data analysis.} The raw MIMIC-IV tables are
profiled for completeness, and the six core physiological features (lactate,
white blood cell count, heart rate, respiratory rate, temperature, and age) are
examined by sepsis status. Figure~\ref{fig:eda_boxplots} shows the distribution
of three features by sepsis status: septic stays exhibit higher lactate, faster
heart rates, and elevated respiratory rates, but with substantial overlap that
motivates multivariable modelling.

\begin{figure}[H]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=0.85\textwidth]{images/06_eda_boxplots.png}
    \caption{Boxplots of heart rate, lactate, and respiratory rate by sepsis
    status. All three features show genuine separation, but no single feature
    cleanly discriminates the groups.}
    \label{fig:eda_boxplots}
\end{figure}

Figure~\ref{fig:lactate_density} shows the lactate distribution by sepsis
status, confirming a right-skewed, overlapping pattern that informs the
choice of log-transformation for distributional models.

\begin{figure}[H]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=0.65\textwidth]{images/06_lactate_density.png}
    \caption{Kernel density estimate of maximum lactate by sepsis status. Both
    groups are right-skewed; the septic distribution is shifted upward but
    overlaps heavily with the non-septic distribution.}
    \label{fig:lactate_density}
\end{figure}

\paragraph{Stage 02: Cohort extraction.} The adult, first-ICU-stay cohort
of 65{,}241 stays is built from the administrative tables, applying the
inclusion criteria of Section~\ref{sec:panel}.

\paragraph{Stage 03: Suspected-infection detection.} The prescription and
microbiology tables are streamed to pair antibiotics with cultures inside the
Sepsis-3 time windows, yielding a binary suspected-infection flag per stay.

\paragraph{Stage 04: Measurement extraction.} The multi-gigabyte chart and
laboratory tables are processed in bounded-memory chunks, extracting the core
vitals and lab values and mapping them to clinical item identifiers.

\paragraph{Stage 05: SOFA computation and Sepsis-3 labelling.} The six
SOFA organ subsystems are scored from the first 24 hours of data, and the
Sepsis-3 rule is applied to produce the binary sepsis label. The resulting
analysis matrix has 65{,}241 rows, of which 37.1\% meet Sepsis-3 criteria.

\paragraph{Stages 07--08: Hourly panel and onset labelling.} Every stay is
resampled onto a regular hourly grid (approximately 3.1 million stay-hours).
Hourly SOFA is scored, and the exact onset hour is located.
Figure~\ref{fig:onset_histogram} shows the distribution of onset hours,
revealing a critical distinction: a large spike at hour 0 marks
\emph{prevalent} sepsis (already present at admission), while the right tail
represents \emph{incident} sepsis developing in the ICU---the target of
early prediction.

\section{Model Layer (Scripts 09--19)}

The model layer implements the seven statistical analyses. Because Analysis~2
(the dynamic landmark supermodel) is the central methodological contribution of
this thesis, it receives the most detailed treatment here.

\subsection{Analysis 1: Full-Feature Elastic-Net Nomogram}
The Analysis-1 nomogram is an elastic-net penalised logistic regression at the
hour-six landmark, using all 27 candidate features: vitals (6), severity
scores (3), labs (4), six-hour trajectory slopes (4), binary ``was measured''
indicators (8, one per lab and per slope), plus age and sex. Nested five-fold cross-validation (outer
folds for honest evaluation, inner folds for $\lambda$ selection) yields
out-of-sample predicted risks.

The key design choice is the \textbf{informative-missingness encoding}: each
laboratory value is median-imputed and accompanied by a binary indicator
recording whether it was measured. At hour 6, labs are missing in 70--88\% of
stays (clinicians order labs selectively in response to suspicion), so these
indicators capture a genuine clinical signal rather than a nuisance pattern.

Figure~\ref{fig:nomogram_h6} shows the clinical nomogram derived from the
elastic-net-selected features, allowing point-based bedside risk estimation.

\subsection{Analysis 2: Dynamic Landmark Supermodel}
\label{sec:supermodel_design}

A single landmark answers ``predict from hour 6.'' A deployed system must
predict at \emph{every} hour. The classical solution is a \textbf{landmark
supermodel} \cite{vanhouwelingen2007landmark}: the landmark construction is
repeated on a grid of prediction hours $s \in \{6, 12, 18, 24, 36, 48\}$, the
resulting datasets are stacked vertically, and a single penalised model is
fitted with $s$, $s^2$, and all 27 base-feature-by-$s$ interactions as
additional covariates (56 candidate features in total). Patient-grouped folds
prevent information leakage across landmarks.

The supermodel design has three important properties:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Parsimony.} One model replaces six separate per-landmark models,
  borrowing strength across prediction times while allowing the feature effects
  to change smoothly with $s$ through the interaction terms.
  \item \textbf{Deployability.} At any ICU hour, the model is evaluated by
  setting $s$ to the current hour and plugging in the patient's current
  features. The output is a continuously updated risk estimate.
  \item \textbf{Interpretability.} The coefficients are directly readable as
  log-odds ratios, and their dependence on $s$ can be inspected through the
  interaction terms.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Analysis 3: Within-Patient Correlation}

The hourly panel contains repeated measurements from the same patient, and
these observations are strongly correlated. A naive pooled logistic model
treats the approximately 76{,}000 at-risk stay-hours as independent, which
produces artificially narrow confidence intervals. Analysis~3 addresses this in
two ways:

\begin{enumerate}
  \item A \textbf{GEE} with an exchangeable working correlation structure
  estimates population-averaged effects with cluster-robust sandwich standard
  errors.
  \item A \textbf{ridge-penalised time-varying Cox model} on the
  counting-process formulation (start-stop intervals, one per hour) provides
  inferentially valid hazard ratios with cluster-robust variance estimation.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Analyses 4--7: Evaluation and Complementary Studies}

\paragraph{Analysis 4 (Real-time evaluation).} The supermodel is trained on
44{,}389 stays and evaluated on a held-out set of 6{,}000 stays (152{,}910
scored hours), computing the PhysioNet/CinC 2019 utility score and an
alarm-burden sweep.

\paragraph{Analysis 5 (Subgroup fairness).} Discrimination and calibration-in-the-large are stratified by sex and four age bands ($<$50, 50--64, 65--79,
80+) to assess equitable performance.

\paragraph{Analysis 6 (GBTM).} A latent-class growth model
\cite{yang2022gbtm} is fitted to first-24h SOFA trajectories via
\texttt{flexmix}, selecting the number of classes by BIC. Class membership is
linked to both mortality (Cox regression) and incident Sepsis-3 (logistic
regression).

\paragraph{Analysis 7 (Causal analysis).} The confounder-adjusted effect of
early antibiotic administration ($\le 3$ hours) on mortality is estimated
using crude, PSM, IPW, and augmented-IPW estimators, with the adjustment set
chosen by LASSO.

\section{Evaluate Layer (Scripts 20--22)}

The evaluate layer consolidates results across all analyses into a single
comprehensive report and performs external validation. The frozen MIMIC
model's coefficients are serialised and applied to eICU-CRD by assembling
the identical feature design matrix from eICU data and computing the
linear predictor directly.

\section{Implementation Notes}
Extraction and panel assembly are implemented in Python with pandas; the
statistical analyses and their reports are implemented in R. All intermediate
artefacts are stored as Parquet (for large panel data) or CSV (for small
result tables). All randomness uses a fixed seed (486649), and the entire
pipeline is reproducible from a single driver script (\texttt{run\_pipeline.py})
that executes the stages in order.
