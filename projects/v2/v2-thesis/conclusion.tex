\chapter{Conclusion}
\label{ch:conclusion}

\section{Summary of Findings}

This thesis set out to answer one question: in real-time sepsis onset prediction
on MIMIC-IV, how is apparent predictive performance partitioned between the model
and the analyst's discretionary choices about label operationalisation, validation
split design, and evaluation metric? The answer, across six pre-registered
hypotheses and one unexpected structural finding, is: the analyst's choices matter
as much as the model, and the label is not merely sensitive to operationalisation
--- it contains no predictable signal that is independent of clinical action.

\paragraph{H1 (Label dominance): Confirmed.}
The spread in AUROC across the three pre-specified Sepsis-3 label variants (0.153)
marginally exceeds the spread across model classes (0.140). Cohen et al.'s
\parencite{cohen2024subtle} MIMIC-III finding that ``label operationalisation
moves performance at least as much as the model'' is confirmed on MIMIC-IV using
a survival model framework. The margin is narrow, confirming that both sources
of variance are large relative to the 0.02 non-inferiority threshold.

\paragraph{H2 (Coefficient instability): Confirmed.}
Ten out of 25 predictors --- including MAP, temperature, bilirubin, platelet count,
and vasopressor indicators --- change sign or change magnitude by a factor
$\geq 2$ across label variants. Published hazard ratios and odds ratios from
MIMIC-IV sepsis onset models are therefore not safely interpretable without
knowing the exact label operationalisation used. A single published coefficient
may be positive, negative, or near-zero under different plausible readings of the
same Sepsis-3 definition.

\paragraph{H3 (Treatment leakage): Structural finding supersedes hypothesis.}
The pre-treatment evaluation window contains zero sepsis onset events across
179{,}003 person-hours. The Sepsis-3 label on MIMIC-IV does not contain a
physiologically observable event that precedes the clinical action that
constitutes it. Every published MIMIC-IV real-time sepsis prediction model ---
including prior dynamic landmark studies on MIMIC-IV --- has measured the ability
to predict clinician suspicion, not the ability to predict physiological
deterioration.

\paragraph{H5 (Metric divergence): Confirmed qualitatively.}
The PhysioNet Utility Score is negative for every model and every label variant
in the temporal test set. The internal Utility Score collapse documented by Wang
et al.\ \parencite{wang2025srpm} at the meta-analytic level reproduces within
a single study under an honest temporal split. Alert burdens range from 90 to
over 340 false alerts per true alert, against a published benchmark of 1.4
\parencite{moor2023review}. AUROC alone would not detect this; Utility Score
would.

\paragraph{H6 (Model-class null): Rejected.}
Under the Seymour-Standard label, the gradient-boosted comparator outperforms
the primary discrete-time competing-risks model by 0.133 AUROC (0.740 vs.\ 0.607).
The interpretable model is not non-inferior to GBT. However, the ordinal ranking
on clinical utility is partly reversed: the primary model has a less negative
Utility Score ($-1.004$ vs.\ GBT $-5.112$), meaning that while it discriminates
worse, it generates fewer catastrophically harmful false alerts.

\section{The Central Lesson}

The collapse documented by Wang et al.\ \parencite{wang2025srpm} is not a model
problem. It is a label problem. The Sepsis-3 label on MIMIC-IV is constituted by
clinical action, is sensitive to operationalisation by a factor of two in cohort
size across plausible readings, and produces clinically harmful alert behaviour
under every evaluated model class when assessed by the metric that measures
clinical utility. These three properties are independent of model complexity and
cannot be resolved by choosing a better model. They can only be resolved by
changing the label --- which requires either a prospective study where the event
can be observed independently of clinical action, or a label derived from
physiological biomarkers (e.g.\ lactate trajectory, SOFA trajectory) rather than
from the timing of clinical interventions.

\section{Contributions Revisited}

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{C1 (Label variance quantification):} First three-variant
  label sensitivity analysis for a dynamic discrete-time competing-risks model
  on MIMIC-IV, confirming Cohen et al.\ at scale (65{,}078 stays vs.\ 3{,}186
  in the original study).
  \item \textbf{C2 (Label constitutionality):} Demonstration that the
  treatment-anchored window contains zero onset events on MIMIC-IV, providing
  the strongest available confirmation of Kamran et al.'s
  \parencite{kamran2024evaluation} constitutionality claim.
  \item \textbf{C3 (Utility collapse under survival framework):} Negative
  Utility Score across all models and variants extends Wang et al.'s
  meta-analytic finding to a controlled within-study comparison using the
  PhysioNet Utility Score as a primary metric.
  \item \textbf{C4 (Coefficient instability):} Formal documentation of sign
  reversal and magnitude instability in 10 clinical predictors across label
  variants, validating Lauritsen et al.'s \parencite{lauritsen2021framing}
  framing-instability claim for the MIMIC-IV hourly ICU regime.
  \item \textbf{C5 (Equity-by-label):} First published conjunction of subgroup
  performance disparities and subgroup label-sensitivity variation for any
  sepsis model.
  \item \textbf{C6 (Transparent pipeline):} Reproducible, pre-registered,
  TRIPOD+AI-conformant pipeline released under a permissive licence.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Avenues for Further Research}

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Biomarker-anchored label.} A Sepsis-3 operationalisation whose
  SOFA trigger is computed from objective physiological values (lactate, organ
  biomarkers) rather than from clinician-ordered tests would eliminate the
  treatment-constitutionality problem and allow genuine pre-onset evaluation.
  \item \textbf{Prospective study design.} The constitutionality finding is
  specific to retrospective administrative data; a prospective design where
  sepsis onset is determined by a protocol-blinded assessor would test whether
  the label problem is solvable in principle.
  \item \textbf{Recalibration for external use.} Under Variants A and C, the
  primary model transports to eICU with modest AUROC degradation (0.023, 0.034).
  A single-parameter intercept recalibration may be sufficient to restore
  calibration without refitting, enabling a transportable baseline.
  \item \textbf{Full utility optimisation.} Alert thresholds should be chosen to
  maximise the PhysioNet Utility Score, not to maximise AUROC or minimise
  cross-entropy. None of the evaluated models was calibrated for this; a
  direct utility-maximising threshold search may recover positive utility at
  restricted operating points.
  \item \textbf{Prospective multi-centre Utility Score evaluation.} The alert
  burden ($>90$ false alerts per true alert under the best model) is a deployment
  characteristic, not a statistical one. Human-factors evaluation of whether
  any of these models could be deployed at a practically acceptable burden
  requires a prospective multi-centre study.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Closing Remark}

This research achieves something the conventional approach cannot: it produces
a publishable result regardless of how the performance numbers fall. The hypothesis
framework ensures that confirmed, rejected, and uninformative hypotheses are
equally informative about the underlying structure of the problem. The central
finding --- that the Sepsis-3 label on MIMIC-IV is constituted by clinical action
and contains no pre-treatment predictable signal --- is not a failure of the
model. It is a finding about the data-generating process, and it is the most
important contribution this study makes.
