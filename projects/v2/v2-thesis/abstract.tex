\chapter*{Abstract}
\addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{Abstract}

Over ninety AI models have been built to predict sepsis in real time, yet they
fail in practice. Wang et al.\ (2025) showed that while AUROC barely changes
between internal and external testing (0.811 to 0.783), the clinical Utility
Score collapses from $+0.381$ to $-0.164$, meaning these models cause more
harm than benefit when deployed. This thesis investigates why: is the problem
the model, the sepsis label, or the researcher's methodological choices?

The study uses MIMIC-IV v3.1 (65,078 adult ICU stays), with external validation
on eICU-CRD v2.0 restricted to the stays in which the Sepsis-3 anchor is
recoverable at all. Three
Sepsis-3 label variants (Narrow, Seymour-Standard, Liberal) are tested with a
dynamic discrete-time competing-risks hazard model, gradient-boosted trees (GBT),
a static Cox model, and rule-based scores (NEWS2, qSOFA, SIRS). Because all
three variants share one culture-plus-antibiotic anchor, the Sepsis-3
conjunction is additionally split into its two limbs and each is labelled
separately, including a deterioration label that uses no treatment timestamp at
all, and Sepsis-3 is relabelled a third time under the standard
$\min(t_{\text{susp}}, t_{\text{SOFA}})$ onset timing of Seymour et al.\ (2016)
and the PhysioNet/CinC 2019 Challenge, which allows an onset to precede
treatment.

The results invert the study's own premise. Model class dominates the
decomposition, moving AUROC by \pcSpreadModelClass{} against the label
definition's \pcSpreadLabel{} --- a difference of \pcHOneEst{} whose interval
[\pcHOneCiLo, \pcHOneCiHi] excludes zero. Yet the three labels disagree sharply
about \emph{which} patients are septic ($\kappa = \pcKappaVsBA$ between the two
narrowest readings, at near-identical prevalence), so label choice sets the
cohort and the denominator of every downstream clinical claim without much
affecting how learnable that cohort is. \pcHTwoEst{} of \pcHTwoNTerms{} shared
covariates are unstable across labels, including a sign reversal on vasopressor
exposure. Random data splits do \emph{not} inflate AUROC relative to time-based
splits (\pcHFourEst, 95\% CI \pcHFourCiLo--\pcHFourCiHi). Discrimination does
fall on transport to eICU-CRD, by \pcHFiveEst{} (95\% CI
\pcHFiveCiLo--\pcHFiveCiHi) --- close to the 0.085 anticipated from the
literature, but estimated on \pcExtNEventsB{} external events, because the
Sepsis-3 anchor is recoverable in only \pcExtMicroCoverage\% of eICU-CRD stays.
The direction is established and the magnitude is not. An interpretable hazard
model matches
gradient-boosted trees to within \pcHSixEst{} AUROC
[\pcHSixCiLo, \pcHSixCiHi], inside the pre-registered equivalence margin. The
pre-treatment evaluation window --- the hours before the first antibiotic or
culture --- contains \textbf{zero sepsis events} in
\pcPreTreatPersonHoursVarB{} person-hours. That zero is proved here to be a
logical consequence of the conjunction-based onset rule rather than a discovery
about the data: when the onset of the \emph{same} labelled stays is timed by the
standard $\min(t_{\text{susp}}, t_{\text{SOFA}})$ rule instead,
\pcFNMovedEarlier{} onsets (\pcFPctMovedEarlier\%) move earlier and
\textbf{\pcPreTreatOnsetsVarF{} fall before treatment begins}, identified at
\pcPreTreatAurocPrimaryF{} AUROC. Sepsis-3 onsets can precede clinical action;
it is the conjunction-based onset rule, widely used in this literature, that
prevents them from being recorded. Sweeping the alert threshold rather than
scoring at an arbitrary cut-off, \textbf{no model exceeds a Utility Score of
\pcUtilityMaxGbtB{}} under the primary label --- a few per cent above the
strategy of issuing no alerts at all, on a scale whose upper bound is 1 --- and
that maximum is bought at \pcUtilityOptBurdenGbtB{} false alerts for every true
one, against a benchmark of 1.4. Under that label four of the six models have
no threshold at which alerting beats silence at all.

Splitting the definition shows how much of the label effect is definitional and
how much is real. The onset \emph{time} under every pre-registered Sepsis-3
variant is fixed entirely by the treatment anchor, the organ-dysfunction
criterion selecting which stays are labelled without moving any onset by an
hour --- an implementation choice, not a property of Sepsis-3. Yet a
treatment-independent deterioration label agrees with Sepsis-3 at
$\kappa = \pcKappaVsBD$, close to chance, so the label effect is not an artefact
of re-reading one definition. The treatment anchor alone is as predictable as
Sepsis-3 built on top of it (anchor-attributable fraction
\pcPhiAnchorPrimary, 95\% CI \pcPhiAnchorCiLoPrimary--\pcPhiAnchorCiHiPrimary,
including 1), so the organ-dysfunction requirement selects which stays are
labelled without selecting the separable ones. Re-timing the onset, by contrast,
does change separability: Variant F is \pcAltDeltaPrimaryF{} AUROC above
Variant B on the same patients. Critically, the deterioration label records
\textbf{\pcPreTreatOnsetsVarD{}
onsets in the pre-treatment window}, at \pcPreTreatAurocPrimaryD{} AUROC:
patients do deteriorate before clinicians act, and the empty window is a
limitation of how the onset was timed rather than a fact about the patients.

All estimates carry stay-level cluster-bootstrap intervals; the six
pre-registered hypotheses are corrected by Holm--Bonferroni and all subgroup
analyses by Benjamini--Hochberg. Under correction, subgroup discrimination
differences by race are too imprecisely estimated to support any claim, while
differential \emph{label} sensitivity does survive: patients whose race is
undocumented, and Spanish-speaking patients, change sepsis status across label
definitions significantly more often than the reference groups.

The collapse in clinical utility is not a model problem, and on this evidence it
is not primarily a discrimination problem either. It is a label problem. Better
models cannot fix a label whose \emph{membership} is constituted by the very
actions it is meant to anticipate --- and how that label's onset is
\emph{timed} determines whether anticipation can be measured at all.

\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} sepsis; MIMIC-IV; discrete-time survival analysis;
competing risks; label definition; variance decomposition; clinical utility;
temporal dataset shift; treatment-anchored labels; TRIPOD+AI.
