\chapter*{Abstract}
\addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{Abstract}

Real-time sepsis onset prediction in the ICU has attracted ninety-one published
models, yet Wang et al.\ (2025) showed that the median clinical Utility Score
collapses from $+0.381$ internally to $-0.164$ externally while AUROC falls only
marginally. This thesis asks whether that collapse is attributable to the model,
to how the Sepsis-3 label is operationalised, or to other analyst-level
methodological choices --- the evaluation metric, the validation split design,
and the temporal anchoring of predictions relative to clinical action.

The study is conducted on MIMIC-IV v3.1 (65{,}078 adult ICU stays) with external
validation on eICU-CRD v2.0 (63 labelled sepsis episodes over 7.9 million
person-hours). Three pre-registered Sepsis-3 onset label variants (Narrow,
Seymour-Standard, Liberal) were extracted and each was used to build a
person-hour table with competing events. The primary model is a dynamic
discrete-time competing-risks hazard model (multinomial pooled logistic regression
over hourly records, equivalent to time-dependent-covariate Cox regression under
short intervals), benchmarked against gradient-boosted trees (GBT), a static Cox
model, and the rule-based scores NEWS2, qSOFA, and SIRS.

The variance decomposition confirms that label operationalisation spreads AUROC by
0.153 across variants, marginally exceeding the model-class spread of 0.140 (H1
confirmed). Ten covariates --- including mean arterial pressure, temperature,
bilirubin, and vasopressor indicators --- change sign or magnitude $\geq 2\times$
across label variants (H2 confirmed), undermining coefficient interpretability.
The pre-registered model-class non-inferiority hypothesis (H6: $|\Delta| \leq
0.02$) is rejected: GBT outperforms the primary model by 0.133 AUROC under the
Seymour-Standard label. Crucially, the treatment-anchored evaluation window
contains \textbf{zero sepsis onset events}, confirming that the Sepsis-3 label is
entirely constituted by clinical action and that current models predict clinician
suspicion rather than physiological deterioration. The PhysioNet Utility Score is
\textbf{negative for every model and every label variant}, meaning that all
evaluated models perform worse than issuing no alert at all. Alert burden exceeds
200 false alerts per true alert across all configurations, compared with the
published benchmark of 1.4.

The thesis concludes that the collapse documented by Wang et al.\ is not a model
problem but a label problem: once the label's constitutive dependence on clinical
action is acknowledged, the question of which model class to use becomes secondary.

\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} sepsis; MIMIC-IV; discrete-time survival analysis;
competing risks; label definition; variance decomposition; clinical utility;
temporal dataset shift; treatment-anchored evaluation; TRIPOD+AI.
