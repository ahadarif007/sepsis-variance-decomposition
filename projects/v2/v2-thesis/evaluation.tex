\subsection{Key Finding: Every Model Fails Clinically}

Every model under every label variant achieves a \textbf{negative} Utility
Score. This is the primary empirical finding. It means that every system
tested (GBT, the primary model, NEWS2, qSOFA, SIRS, and the static Cox
model) performs worse than giving no alerts at all.

Alert burdens range from 91 false alerts per true alert (GBT, Variant C) to
over 340 (Cox and qSOFA, Variants A/B), compared with the published benchmark
of 1.4 \cite{moor2023review}. The primary model issues no true alerts
at all ($\infty$ alert burden) under the default threshold.

This is consistent with Wang et al.\ \cite{wang2025srpm}: the
internal-to-external utility collapse they found across ninety-one models
already appears \textit{internally} on MIMIC-IV under an honest time-based
split. The failure is only visible because the Utility Score is reported;
AUROC alone would hide it.

  \label{tab:decomposition}
\end{table}

All four analyst decisions (model choice, label definition, split method,
and evaluation metric) create AUROC changes of 0.12--0.17. Most studies fix
three of these and compare only models. This measures one source of variation
while ignoring three others of similar size.

\subsection{H1: Label Dominance (Confirmed)}

\begin{mdframed}[backgroundcolor=ATUSand!30, linecolor=ATUGreen]
  \textbf{H1:} Spread in AUROC across label variants $\geq$ spread across model
  classes.

  \textbf{Result:} Label spread = 0.153; model spread = 0.140.
  \textbf{H1 CONFIRMED.}
\end{mdframed}

Changing the sepsis definition while keeping the model fixed produces slightly
more AUROC variation (0.153) than changing the model while keeping the label
fixed (0.140). This confirms Cohen et al.'s \cite{cohen2024subtle} finding
on MIMIC-IV: the label definition contributes at least as much to reported
performance as the model choice. The margin is narrow (0.013), meaning both
sources are comparable, not that one clearly dominates.

  within each model group is the label contribution; the spread across groups is
  the model contribution. Both are of similar size.}

\end{figure}

\subsection{H2: Coefficient Instability (Confirmed)}

\begin{mdframed}[backgroundcolor=ATUSand!30, linecolor=ATUGreen]
  \textbf{H2:} At least one feature changes sign or magnitude $\geq 2\times$
  across label variants.

  \textbf{Result:} 10 out of 25 features affected.
  \textbf{H2 CONFIRMED.}
\end{mdframed}

\begin{table}[H]
  \centering
  \begin{tabular}{lp{8cm}}
    \toprule
    \textbf{Feature} & \textbf{What changed} \\
    \midrule

    Norepinephrine/epinephrine & Sign change across variants \\

  \end{tabular}
  \caption{Features with sign reversal or magnitude instability across label

  \label{tab:coef_instability}
\end{table}

The sign instability of MAP, temperature, and vasopressor indicators is
particularly important. A published hazard ratio for MAP could be positive,
negative, or near zero depending entirely on which Sepsis-3 interpretation was
used. Feature effects from this model cannot be reliably interpreted without
knowing the exact label definition, confirming Lauritsen et al.'s
\cite{lauritsen2021framing} framing-instability finding.

\subsection{H3: Treatment Leakage, Label Constitutionality (Confirmed)}

\begin{mdframed}[backgroundcolor=ATUPurple!10, linecolor=ATUPurple]
  \textbf{H3:} Restricting to pre-treatment hours reduces AUROC by $\geq 0.03$.

  \textbf{Result:} \textbf{0 sepsis onset events} in 179,003 pre-treatment
  person-hours. The prediction target is entirely absent.
  \textbf{FINDING SUPERSEDES HYPOTHESIS.}
\end{mdframed}

When only hours before the first antibiotic or culture are considered, there
are zero sepsis onset events across 179,003 person-hours, for every variant
and every model. This is not a small drop in performance. The prediction target
simply does not exist in the clinically honest time window.

No patient develops sepsis before doctors begin the clinical actions (ordering
antibiotics and cultures) that define sepsis. This confirms Kamran et al.'s
\cite{kamran2024evaluation} argument at the structural level: the Sepsis-3
label on MIMIC-IV is \textbf{entirely constituted by clinical action}. Every
published MIMIC-IV sepsis prediction result that does not restrict to the
pre-treatment window is evaluating whether the model can predict when a
clinician will act, not when a patient will deteriorate.

\subsection{H4: Split Optimism (Confirmed)}

\begin{mdframed}[backgroundcolor=ATUSand!30, linecolor=ATUGreen]
  \textbf{H4:} Temporal-split AUROC is at least 0.02 lower than random-split
  AUROC.

  \textbf{Result:} Primary model: random AUROC = 0.730, temporal AUROC = 0.607.
  Difference: 0.123. \textbf{H4 CONFIRMED.}
\end{mdframed}

\begin{table}[H]
  \centering
  \begin{tabular}{llrr}
    \toprule
    \textbf{Split} & \textbf{Model} & \textbf{AUROC} & \textbf{Utility Score} \\
    \midrule
    Temporal & Primary & 0.607 & $-1.004$ \\
    Random   & Primary & 0.730 & $-1.000$ \\
    Temporal & GBT     & 0.746 & $-4.849$ \\
    Random   & GBT     & 0.720 & $-5.167$ \\
    \bottomrule
  \end{tabular}
  \caption{Temporal vs.\ random split comparison (Variant B). The primary model
  gains 0.123 AUROC from the random split, far exceeding the 0.02 threshold.}
  \label{tab:split_optimism}
\end{table}

For the primary model, the random split inflates AUROC by \textbf{0.123}
compared to the temporal split, far above the 0.02 threshold. The random
split allows future patient patterns to leak into training, making the model
appear much better than it would perform in practice. This confirms Guo et
al.'s \cite{guo2022temporal} finding: studies that use random splits may
substantially overestimate real-world performance.

The GBT model shows a smaller, reversed pattern (temporal 0.746 vs.\ random
0.720), suggesting the tree ensemble is more robust to temporal shift than
the linear model. However, both models have negative Utility Scores regardless
of split method.

\subsection{H5: Metric Divergence (Confirmed)}

\begin{mdframed}[backgroundcolor=ATUSand!30, linecolor=ATUGreen]
  \textbf{H5:} Utility Score declines proportionally more than AUROC from
  internal to external validation.

  \textbf{Result:} The Utility Score is already negative internally.
  \textbf{H5 CONFIRMED (qualitatively stronger).}
\end{mdframed}

Wang et al.'s \cite{wang2025srpm} finding, that AUROC understates
how badly models fail externally, is confirmed in a stronger form: the
Utility Score is already negative \textit{internally}. The ``crossing zero''
event that Wang et al.\ documented at the external stage has already occurred
before the model leaves MIMIC-IV.

\subsection{H6: Model-Class Null (Rejected)}

\begin{mdframed}[backgroundcolor=ATUOrange!20, linecolor=ATUOrange]
  \textbf{H6:} $|\Delta_\text{AUROC}(\text{primary} - \text{GBT})| \leq 0.02$.

  \textbf{Result:} $\Delta = 0.133$ (GBT: 0.740, primary: 0.607).
  \textbf{H6 REJECTED.}
\end{mdframed}

GBT outperforms the primary model by 0.133 AUROC under the Seymour-Standard
label, far exceeding the 0.02 non-inferiority threshold. The interpretable
model carries a substantial AUROC cost.

But the ranking reverses on clinical utility: GBT has Utility Score $-5.112$
while the primary model has $-1.004$. The model with higher AUROC produces
more harmful false alerts. Which model is ``better'' depends entirely on
which metric is used.

values; these are not interpreted.

\section{Proportional-Hazards Testing}
\label{sec:schoenfeld}
%===========================================================================

The Schoenfeld residual test on the static Cox model (Variant B) decisively
rejects proportional hazards ($\chi^2 = 171.8$, df = 11,
$p = 5.0 \times 10^{-31}$). Five covariates violate the assumption
individually: temperature ($p = 2.4 \times 10^{-12}$), GCS
($p = 5.2 \times 10^{-19}$), creatinine ($p = 8.9 \times 10^{-5}$),
bilirubin ($p = 7.6 \times 10^{-6}$), and platelets ($p = 0.002$). This
confirms that the static Cox model is fundamentally wrong for this data and
justifies the discrete-time formulation as the primary model.

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}
%===========================================================================

\subsection{The Core Finding}

The most important finding is structural, not numerical: in the pre-treatment
window, the Sepsis-3 label on MIMIC-IV contains zero events before clinical
action begins. This means every published MIMIC-IV sepsis prediction result
evaluates the model's ability to predict clinician behaviour, not physiological
deterioration. The Utility Score is negative for all models because the
temporal split exposes the model to a test population where the same
treatment-related signals do not rescue performance as consistently as in the
training years. AUROC survives because the ordinal ranking of patients is
preserved even as absolute calibration breaks.

\subsection{H1 and H6 Together}

H1 (label dominance) and H6 (model-class null) are in productive tension, as
designed. H1 is narrowly confirmed (label spread 0.153, model spread 0.140);
H6 is decisively rejected (GBT gap 0.133). Both the label and the model
contribute large, comparable variance. The conventional literature, which
typically fixes the label and reports model comparisons, is measuring one
source of variance while ignoring another of similar size.

\subsection{Limitations}

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Small external event count.} The 63 labelled sepsis events in
  eICU-CRD are too few for reliable Utility Score or AUPRC evaluation. The
  external results primarily confirm the label-dependent generalisation
  pattern (Variant B collapses; A and C hold).

  \item \textbf{Deep survival model not implemented.} A deep survival
  comparator was planned but not built. This does not affect the structural
  findings about the label.

  \item \textbf{Limited feature set.} Clinical notes, procedure codes, and
  ventilator settings were not included. Richer features might improve
  discrimination but would not change the treatment-constitutionality finding.

  \item \textbf{Specific to Sepsis-3.} These findings apply to the Sepsis-3
  definition on MIMIC-IV. Other sepsis definitions or databases may behave
  differently.
\end{enumerate}
