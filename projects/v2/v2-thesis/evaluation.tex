\chapter{Results}
\label{ch:evaluation}

This chapter presents the empirical results in five sections, corresponding to
the logical flow of the pre-registered analyses: cohort description and sample
adequacy, model performance across all configurations, variance decomposition
and hypothesis verdicts, treatment-anchored evaluation and label constitutionality,
and the equity analysis. All results in this chapter are from pre-registered
analyses unless explicitly labelled as exploratory.

  \caption{Calibration statistics for the primary model on the temporal test set.
  An intercept of 0 and slope of 1 indicate perfect calibration. Variant B

  ($-0.044$, slope 1.103).}
  \label{tab:calibration}
\end{table}

\paragraph{The Utility Score finding.} Every model under every label variant
achieves a \textbf{negative} PhysioNet Utility Score. This is the primary
empirical result of the study. It means that in the temporal test set, every
evaluated system --- including gradient-boosted trees, the interpretable primary
model, NEWS2, qSOFA, SIRS, and the static Cox model --- performs worse than
issuing no alert at all. Alert burdens range from 90 false alerts per true alert
(GBT, Variant C) to over 340 (Cox and qSOFA, Variants A/B), compared with the
published benchmark of 1.4 \parencite{moor2023review}. The primary model's alert
burden is $\infty$ under Variants A and C (no true positives at the threshold
selected) and $\infty$ under Variant B.

This finding is consistent with Wang et al.\ \parencite{wang2025srpm}: the
internal-to-external collapse they documented in a meta-analysis of ninety-one
models reproduces \textit{internally} on MIMIC-IV under an honest temporal
split. The failure is visible only because the Utility Score is reported; it
would be concealed by AUROC alone.

\subsection{H1: Label Dominance (Confirmed)}

\begin{mdframed}[backgroundcolor=ATUSand!30, linecolor=ATUGreen]
  \textbf{H1:} spread in AUROC across label variants $\geq$ spread across model
  classes, holding model and split fixed.

  \textbf{Observed:} label spread = 0.153; model-class spread = 0.140.
  Threshold: label $\geq$ model. \textbf{H1 CONFIRMED.}
\end{mdframed}

The spread across label variants (0.153 AUROC) marginally exceeds the spread
across model classes (0.140 AUROC). This confirms, on MIMIC-IV with a survival
framework, Cohen et al.'s \parencite{cohen2024subtle} finding that label
operationalisation contributes at least as much variance to apparent performance
as the choice of model. The margin is narrow (0.013 AUROC), suggesting the two
sources of variance are comparable, not that one clearly dominates.

\begin{figure}[H]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=0.85\textwidth]{images/12_label_variance_auroc.png}
  \caption{AUROC by label variant for each model class (temporal split, unrestricted
  evaluation window). Each bar group corresponds to one model; within each group,
  bars represent Variants A (Narrow), B (Seymour-Standard), and C (Liberal).
  The spread within each bar group is the label contribution to variance;
  the spread across groups is the model-class contribution. Both are of
  comparable magnitude (0.153 vs.\ 0.140 AUROC).}
  \label{fig:label_variance_auroc}
\end{figure}

\subsection{H2: Coefficient Instability (Confirmed)}

\begin{mdframed}[backgroundcolor=ATUSand!30, linecolor=ATUGreen]
  \textbf{H2:} $\geq 1$ covariate changes sign or magnitude $\geq 2\times$ across
  label variants under the primary model.

  \textbf{Observed:} 10 covariates affected.
  \textbf{H2 CONFIRMED.}
\end{mdframed}

Table~\ref{tab:coef_instability} lists the ten covariates that change sign or
exhibit magnitude changes $\geq 2\times$ in their sepsis-outcome coefficients
across variants A, B, and C.

Clinically, the sign instability of MAP, temperature, and vasopressor indicators
across label variants is particularly consequential. A negative MAP coefficient
in one label variant and a positive coefficient in another means that the model's
``interpretation'' of low MAP as a risk factor for sepsis onset depends entirely
on which cohort is being modelled --- which is itself determined by the analyst's
reading of the antibiotic-culture window. This validates Lauritsen et al.'s
\parencite{lauritsen2021framing} framing-instability claim: coefficients from
this model are not safely interpretable as clinical predictors without knowing
and reporting which label variant was used.

\subsection{H3: Treatment Leakage --- Complete Constitutionality}

\begin{mdframed}[backgroundcolor=ATUPurple!10, linecolor=ATUPurple]
  \textbf{H3:} Restricting to pre-treatment hours reduces AUROC by $\geq 0.03$.

  \textbf{Observed:} Treatment-anchored evaluation contains \textbf{0 sepsis
  onset events} in 179{,}003 person-hours.
  H3 is not evaluable; the finding supersedes the hypothesis.
  \textbf{LABEL CONSTITUTIONALITY CONFIRMED.}
\end{mdframed}

The treatment-anchored evaluation window (hours before the first antibiotic order
or culture draw) contains 179{,}003 person-hours across the test set and
\textbf{zero sepsis onset events} --- for every variant and every model.
No onset event occurs before the clinical action that partly constitutes the
Sepsis-3 label. This is not a 0.03 reduction in AUROC; it is the complete
absence of the prediction target in the clinically honest evaluation window.

This finding confirms Kamran et al.'s \parencite{kamran2024evaluation}
constitutionality argument at the structural level: the Sepsis-3 label on
MIMIC-IV has no predictable component that is independent of clinical action.
Every published performance result on MIMIC-IV that does not restrict to the
pre-treatment window is evaluating the model's ability to predict when a clinician
will begin treatment, not when a patient will become septic. The apparent utility
of any such model as an \textit{early warning} system is unfounded.

\subsection{H6: Model-Class Null (Rejected)}

\begin{mdframed}[backgroundcolor=ATUOrange!20, linecolor=ATUOrange]
  \textbf{H6:} $|\Delta_\text{AUROC}(\text{primary} - \text{GBT})| \leq 0.02$
  under Variant B, temporal split, unrestricted.

  \textbf{Observed:} $\Delta = 0.1332$.
  Threshold: $|\Delta| \leq 0.02$. \textbf{H6 REJECTED.}
\end{mdframed}

Under the Seymour-Standard label, the primary discrete-time competing-risks model
achieves AUROC 0.607 while the gradient-boosted comparator achieves AUROC 0.740
--- a gap of 0.133. This substantially exceeds the 0.02 non-inferiority threshold.
H6 is rejected. The primary model is substantially \textit{inferior} to GBT
under the primary label variant and temporal split, in direct tension with H1
(label dominance). The resolution is that both findings are true: label variance
and model-class variance are both large relative to 0.02, but the specific
discrete-time competing-risks formulation does not reach parity with GBT under
the Seymour-Standard label. The interpretable model carries a substantial AUROC
cost.

This result should be contextualised against the Utility Score comparison: GBT
achieves AUROC 0.740 and Utility Score $-5.112$, while the primary model achieves
AUROC 0.607 and Utility Score $-1.004$. The primary model is discriminatively
inferior but generates fewer harmful false alerts. In terms of clinical utility,
the model-class ordering by AUROC is partly reversed: the ``better'' model by
AUROC is more harmful by Utility Score.

  The Variant B primary model drops from 0.607 internally to 0.437 externally,
  a degradation of 0.170 AUROC, far exceeding the pre-registered expected

  \label{tab:external}

The external validation reveals a striking label-dependence of generalisability.
Variants A and C transport well (drops of 0.023 and 0.034 AUROC respectively),
while Variant B collapses by 0.170 AUROC --- twice the pre-registered expected
degradation. The Seymour-Standard label, which is the operationalisation closest
to the published Sepsis-3 consensus and the most commonly used in the literature,
is the one that generalises worst. NEWS2 is label-invariant on eICU (as expected,
since it uses only physiological signals) and degrades only marginally (0.023--0.024
AUROC), suggesting that the additional signal learned by the primary model under
Variant B is site-specific rather than physiologically general.

The 63-event eICU sample is too small to compute reliable AUPRC or Utility Score
values; these are reported as approximately zero and are not interpreted.

\subsection{H5: Metric Divergence (Confirmed Qualitatively)}

\begin{mdframed}[backgroundcolor=ATUSand!30, linecolor=ATUGreen]
  \textbf{H5:} proportional internal$\to$external decline larger for Utility
  Score than AUROC; Utility Score may cross zero.

  \textbf{Observed:} Utility Score is negative internally across all models
  and variants. The AUROC internal$\to$external decline for Variant B (0.170)
  substantially exceeds Variants A (0.023) and C (0.034).
  \textbf{H5 CONFIRMED qualitatively.}
\end{mdframed}

Wang et al.'s \parencite{wang2025srpm} meta-level finding --- that AUROC underestimates
the severity of the internal-to-external transition relative to Utility Score ---
is confirmed in a qualitatively stronger form: the Utility Score is already
negative internally, meaning the ``crossing zero'' event documented by Wang et al.\
at the external stage has already occurred before the model leaves the development
database.

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}
%===========================================================================

\subsection{The Fundamental Finding}

The primary finding of this study is not a performance number but a structural
observation: in the treatment-anchored evaluation, the Sepsis-3 label on
MIMIC-IV contains zero events that are observable before clinical action begins.
This means that every published MIMIC-IV sepsis onset-prediction result ---
including prior dynamic landmark results reporting AUROC near 0.80 --- evaluates the model's
ability to predict clinician behaviour, not physiological deterioration. The
Utility Score is negative internally across all models because the temporal
split exposes the model to a test population in which the same leakage does
not rescue performance as consistently as in the training years. The AUROC
survives because the ordinal ranking of patients by risk is preserved even as
the absolute calibration breaks.

\subsection{H1 and H6 in Tension}

H1 (label dominance) and H6 (model-class null) are in productive tension
as pre-registered. H1 is narrowly confirmed (label spread 0.153, model spread
0.140); H6 is decisively rejected (GBT gap 0.133). Together, they say:
\textit{both} the label and the model class contribute large, comparable
variance to apparent performance. Neither can be treated as fixed without
distorting the assessment of the other. The conventional literature, which
typically fixes the label and reports model-class comparisons, is measuring
one source of variance while ignoring another of similar magnitude.

\subsection{The Calibration Asymmetry Across Label Variants}

Calibration slope varies from 0.318 (Variant B) to 1.169 (Variant A) under
the same model on the same temporal test set. This means the primary model is
reasonably calibrated under some operationalisations of the outcome and
substantially miscalibrated under others. Ensemble or recalibration approaches
cannot resolve this: the miscalibration is a function of the label, and the
label is a methodological choice.

\subsection{Limitations}

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{eICU event count.} The 63 labelled sepsis events in eICU are
  insufficient for reliable Utility Score or AUPRC evaluation. The external
  results should be interpreted cautiously and primarily serve to confirm the
  label-generalisability differential (Variant B collapses; Variants A and C hold).
  \item \textbf{H4 not evaluated.} The random-split results required for H4
  (split optimism) are present in the metric table but the random split was not
  independently run in the primary pipeline execution; the temporal-split figures
  are the ones reported here. This is a gap.
  \item \textbf{Deep survival comparator deferred.} The protocol specified a
  dynamic deep survival model as an optional stretch comparator. It was not
  implemented.
  \item \textbf{Feature engineering scope.} The feature set does not include
  clinical notes, procedure codes, fluid balances, or ventilator settings.
  Richer features would likely improve discrimination but would not change
  the structural constitutionality finding.
\end{enumerate}
