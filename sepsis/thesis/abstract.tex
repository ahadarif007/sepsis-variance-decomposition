\vspace*{-3\baselineskip}

Over ninety AI models have been built to predict sepsis in real time, yet they
fail in practice: Wang et al.\ (2025) report a clinical Utility Score falling
from $+0.381$ internally to $-0.164$ externally while AUROC barely moves. This
thesis asks whether that failure belongs to the model, to the sepsis label, or
to the analyst's discretionary choices.

Using MIMIC-IV v3.1 (65,078 adult ICU stays) with external validation on
eICU-CRD v2.0, three pre-registered Sepsis-3 label variants are crossed with
five model classes, two split designs and two evaluation metrics, under
stay-level cluster-bootstrap intervals with Holm and Benjamini--Hochberg
correction. Because all three variants share one culture-plus-antibiotic
anchor, the conjunction is additionally split into its limbs --- including a
deterioration label using no treatment timestamp --- and re-timed under the
standard $\min(t_{\text{susp}}, t_{\text{SOFA}})$ onset rule.

The results invert the study's premise. Model class dominates the
decomposition, moving AUROC by \pcSpreadModelClass{} against the label's
\pcSpreadLabel{} on an interval excluding zero; none of the four testable
hypotheses is confirmed and two fail in the direction opposite to the one
predicted. An interpretable hazard model matches gradient-boosted trees to
within \pcHSixEst{} AUROC, and random splits do not inflate AUROC relative to
temporal ones. Discrimination does degrade on transport, but the Sepsis-3
anchor is recoverable in only \pcExtMicroCoverage\% of eICU-CRD stays, so the
direction is established and the magnitude is not.

The label's consequence lies elsewhere. No model exceeds a Utility Score of
\pcUtilityMaxGbtB{} at any threshold, on a scale bounded by 1; the three
variants agree at only $\kappa = \pcKappaVsBA{}$ on which patients are septic,
so the label fixes the cohort and the denominator of every clinical quantity
computed on it. And the pre-treatment window contains zero Sepsis-3 events ---
proved here to follow from the conjunction-based onset rule rather than
discovered in the data. Re-timing the \emph{same} labelled stays by the
standard rule places \pcPreTreatOnsetsVarF{} onsets before treatment begins;
the treatment-independent label places \pcPreTreatOnsetsVarD{} there.

Better models cannot repair a label whose \emph{membership} is constituted by
the actions it is meant to anticipate, and whose onset \emph{timing} decides
whether anticipation is measurable at all.
