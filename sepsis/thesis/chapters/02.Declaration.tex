\chapter*{Declaration}

I declare that this thesis is my own work, that it has not been submitted for
any other academic award, and that all sources consulted are acknowledged in
the text and listed in the references. The research question, the
pre-registered hypotheses, the study design and the interpretation of the
findings are my own, as are the decisions recorded as Protocol Amendments
1--10 and the judgements about what the evidence does and does not support.

\section*{Use of Generative AI}

During this project I applied the PAIR framework to use generative AI
responsibly.

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Problem:} building a complete pipeline for real-time sepsis
    onset prediction required cohort extraction and labelling under several
    Sepsis-3 readings, person-hour panel construction, model implementation
    across six model specifications, an external-validation arm, a clinical-metrics
    and inference layer, and the documentation of all of it.

    \item \textbf{AI:} I used Claude. Because ``AI assistance'' covers
    activities with very different implications for authorship, the uses are
    separated rather than summarised. \textbf{Coding:} discussing
    implementation choices, and refactoring early prototypes into the
    twelve-stage structure presented here. \textbf{Debugging and code
    auditing:} adversarial review of the pipeline against its own
    specification, which is how several of the defects recorded in the
    amendment log were found. \textbf{Thesis prose:} drafting and editing
    wording in this document. \textbf{Literature discovery:} locating and
    summarising candidate sources, each of which I then read and verified
    against the claim it is cited for. \textbf{Statistical reasoning:}
    discussing inference choices, among them the multiplicity plan and the
    interval-inversion reading of H6. In the last three the assistance was to
    the drafting and the discussion; the claims, the citations and the
    decisions are mine, and are the subject of the next item.

    \item \textbf{Interaction:} all generated output was critically reviewed,
    modified and adapted to fit this study's design. The research question, the
    pre-registered hypotheses, the label definitions, the model choices, the
    configuration values, the decisions recorded as Protocol Amendments 1--10
    and the reported results are my own.

    \item \textbf{Reflection:} this approach improved development speed and
    documentation quality while keeping the experimental design, the analysis
    and the final conclusions my own work.
\end{itemize}

Generative AI was \emph{not} used to generate data, to produce or alter any
result, or to decide what the results mean. Every quantity this study estimates
(every AUROC, interval, p-value, count, rate and calibration statistic) is
written into this document mechanically from the pipeline's own output tables
by \texttt{thesis\_constants.R} (Appendix~\ref{app:repro}), and a quantity the
pipeline did not produce typesets as a visible marker rather than as a
plausible number. That mechanism guarantees the \emph{values}, and only the
values. It cannot check that a sentence still describes what the value now
shows, that a section's status tag matches the register, or that a claim has
not outlived the analysis it was written for; those are human obligations, and
the checkers described in Appendix~\ref{app:repro} close only part of the gap. Three classes of number are typed rather than generated, and
none of them is a result of this study: figures quoted from prior work, each
carrying its citation; thresholds and design constants fixed by the
pre-registration before any modelling, such as the equivalence margin and the
correction levels; and, in running prose, values rounded for readability from
the generated macro that states them exactly in the adjacent table. The
analytical claims, the hypothesis verdicts and the limitations are mine, and I
take full responsibility for the content of this thesis.
