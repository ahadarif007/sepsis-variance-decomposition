### 1.3 Design rationale — why this is *not* a PRISMA flow diagram

An earlier draft carried a PRISMA flow diagram populated with `TODO`
placeholders and cited a `search_strategy_queries.xlsx` that did not exist. Both
were removed.

The review is a **structured narrative critical review, not a PRISMA-conformant
systematic review**, and the thesis says so in three separate places (the
§Search Strategy preamble, the figure caption, and Limitation 11). Two PRISMA
requirements were genuinely not met: no title-and-abstract screening pass over a
full de-duplicated yield, and no second independent reviewer. Producing a
diagram shaped like PRISMA with numbers that do not mean what a PRISMA reader
would assume is worse than not having one — it converts a documentation gap into
a misrepresentation.

What replaced it is a **search-and-verification flow figure** (`fig:prisma`)
that follows PRISMA 2020's *reporting logic* — an auditable count at every stage
— under four phases (Identification 35 searches / 15 sources → Selection base
n = 19 → Verification n = 46, 4 excluded, 1 caveated → Included n = 50, 33
cited). Every count is real and traceable.

**Rule going forward:** do not reinstate a PRISMA diagram unless real screening
counts exist.

### 1.4 What is still bounded

Limitation 11 states it plainly: the C1 and C5 novelty claims are bounded by the
coverage this method achieves — deliberate depth on the methodological
literature that constrains the study, not exhaustive coverage of the MIMIC-IV
sepsis corpus. A systematic review could surface a prior study combining
multi-variant label sensitivity with subgroup equity analysis, and this search
would not necessarily have found it. Making that limit explicit is what makes
the hedge honest rather than decorative.

## 2. Label finding — definitional circularity

**This was the deepest change in the response, and the highest-yield one.**

### 2.1 Feedback

Sepsis-3 onset is anchored to clinician actions, so "labels matter" is partly
tautological. Acknowledge the circularity, quantify how much performance is
attributable to the treatment anchor, and ideally test at least one alternative
labelling independent of treatment timing.

The gap was real. Variants A/B/C are three readings of *one* definition and all
three fire on the same culture-plus-antibiotic trigger. The pre-registration
named SEP-1 and CDC Adult Sepsis Event as alternatives; neither was implemented.

### 2.2 What changed — Protocol Amendment 2 (2026-08-02)

Sepsis-3 is a **conjunction**: suspected infection **AND** acute organ
dysfunction. The amendment splits the conjunction and labels each limb alone.

| ID | Name | Suspicion anchor | Organ dysfunction | Uses treatment timing? |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| B | Seymour-Standard | required | required | yes |
| E | Anchor-Only | required | **not** required | yes |
| D | Deterioration | **not** required | required | **no** |

- **Variant E (Anchor-Only)** — onset is the suspicion time itself, SOFA gate
  removed.
- **Variant D (Deterioration)** — onset is the first hour after hour 1 with SOFA
  ≥ 2 over the admission baseline, infection anchor removed **and vasopressor
  terms stripped**, so the label contains no treatment timestamp anywhere.

Implementation: `02_cohort_labels.Rmd` (5 variants), propagated through
`03`, `05`, `06`, `07`, and attributed in `09_variance_decomposition.Rmd`. New
figure `09_label_anchor_attribution.png`. Prose in `methodology.tex` §Beyond
Sepsis-3 (`sec:alt_labels`), `evaluation.tex` §Label-Anchor Attribution
(`sec:label_anchor_results`), the abstract, contribution C7, and Limitations 5–7.

### 2.3 Results

B, D and E all run on the same 65,078 stays and the same temporal split.

| Variant | Onsets ≤72 h | κ vs B | Exact onset match | Primary AUROC | GBT AUROC |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| E Anchor-Only | 17,251 (26.5%) | 0.432 | **100%** | 0.756 | 0.766 |
| B Seymour-Std | 5,887 (9.0%) | — | — | 0.607 | 0.742 |
| D Deterioration | 27,195 (41.8%) | **0.057** | **6.4%** | 0.842 | 0.915 |

**Two structural facts, not estimates:**

1. **E ⊇ B exactly, and onset times are identical in 100% of B's stays.** Onset
   under A/B/C *is* `t_susp`; the SOFA gate only filters *which* stays qualify,
   never *when*. So **100% of Sepsis-3 onset timing is treatment timing.** A vs B
   exact-onset match is 97.5%, C vs B 98.6% — the pre-registered label variance
   is entirely about which patients, never about when.
2. **D vs B κ = 0.057** (near chance), 6.4% exact onset match, median |onset
   shift| 9 h (IQR −11 to +7). A treatment-independent label is a **different
   construct**, not a re-reading of the same one. This is what establishes that
   the label-sensitivity finding is **not purely tautological**.

**Anchor attribution.** φ_anchor = (AUROC_E − 0.5) / (AUROC_B − 0.5):

| Model | AUROC_B | AUROC_E | φ_anchor | 95% CI |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Primary | 0.607 | 0.756 | **2.40** | 2.06 – 2.89 |
| GBT | 0.742 | 0.766 | **1.10** | 1.04 – 1.17 |

Both CIs lie entirely above 1, and φ > 1 for all six models. **Sepsis-3 is
harder than either of its limbs.** The anchor is the predictable part; the SOFA
gate discards two thirds of anchor-positive stays and takes the separable signal
with it.

**The strongest result in the thesis.** In the treatment-anchored (pre-treatment)
evaluation window:

| Variant | Person-hours | Onsets | AUROC (primary / GBT) |
|---|---|---|---|
| B Seymour-Std | 179,003 | **0** | undefined |
| E Anchor-Only | 179,003 | **0** | undefined (structural tautology: E's onset *is* the anchor, and B ⊆ E) |
| D Deterioration | 147,357 | **1,362** | 0.840 / 0.937 |

Patients **do** deteriorate before clinicians act, and that deterioration is
highly predictable. Sepsis-3 simply cannot place an onset there. This **sharpens**
H3 and contribution C2 rather than weakening them: the H3 zero is a property of
the *label*, not of the patients.

**Family E3** (m = 4, Benjamini-Hochberg) — all four ΔAUROC-vs-B contrasts
significant at the smallest reportable p:

| Variant | Model | ΔAUROC vs B | 95% CI | BH significant |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| D | primary | +0.235 | 0.216 – 0.255 | yes |
| E | primary | +0.149 | 0.131 – 0.168 | yes |
| D | gbt | +0.173 | 0.155 – 0.189 | yes |
| E | gbt | +0.024 | 0.009 – 0.038 | yes |

Utility stays negative under D and E (best −0.218, GBT/D). D's best alert burden
is 17.7:1 vs B's 201.7:1 — better, still far from the 1.4 benchmark.

### 2.4 Design rationale

**Why D and E rather than SEP-1, CDC ASE, or PhysioNet/CinC 2019.** All three
were considered and **rejected as the treatment-independent comparator**: SEP-1
and CDC ASE are defined on antibiotic days, and the CinC 2019 label is a
Sepsis-3 construction. **None of them breaks the circularity** — adopting any
would have produced a fourth treatment-anchored label and answered nothing.
Implementing them for cohort-level comparison remains genuine future work and is
recorded as such in Limitation 7, not quietly dropped.

**Why D and E are quarantined from H1.** They live in `ALT_LABEL_VARIANTS`,
never in `LABEL_VARIANTS`; their metrics write to separate files
(`07_metric_results_altlabel.csv`, `09_label_anchor_attribution.csv`,
`12_altlabel_*.csv`). H1, the variance decomposition, and families E1/E2 are
computed from unchanged inputs and were verified byte-identical to the
pre-amendment run by diff. The amendment is post-hoc and is disclosed as
post-hoc; folding it into a pre-registered hypothesis would have destroyed the
thing that makes the pre-registration worth having.

**The caveat that must accompany every D number.** D's label is a threshold
function of recorded physiology, and 5 of its 6 SOFA components are model
inputs. Its discrimination *level* is therefore partly self-fulfilling and **not
comparable** with B's. What carries weight is the κ/onset-shift disagreement and
the 1,362-vs-0 event count — those are properties of the label, not results of a
model. Limitation 6 states this.

### 3.3 Results and the two analytical decisions that mattered

**Calibration.** The primary discrete-time model calibrates well (Variant A:
intercept −0.087, slope 1.029, ICI 0.00026, scaled Brier +0.0036). GBT and Cox
are catastrophically miscalibrated on the probability scale (Variant A GBT:
intercept −6.09, ICI 0.398, **scaled Brier −95.7**). GBT's AUROC advantage does
not survive contact with calibration — which is the point of reporting both.

> **Trap — calibration floor must be 1e-6, not 1e-3.** At a 0.2% hourly event
> rate the conventional 1e-3 clamp overwrites 45–72% of all predictions. This
> alone moved Variant A's slope 1.169 → 1.029 and Variant B's 0.318 → 0.209.
> The conventional default silently manufactures the calibration result.

**Decision curves.** At a threshold equal to prevalence, the primary model
attains 38.0% (Variant A) / 15.0% (Variant B) / 39.9% (Variant C) of the
attainable net benefit. GBT's standardised net benefit is **exactly 0** at
prevalence — it alerts on 100% of hours, which is identical to treat-all.

> **Trap — DCA threshold grid must be 0.1×–10× prevalence, not 0.01–0.20.** The
> conventional grid sits 5–100× above an hourly sepsis prevalence and yields a
> trivially null decision curve for every model. The grid is now anchored to the
> event rate and the reasoning is stated in the text.

**Operating points at 80% per-hour sensitivity.** PPV ranges **0.0021 – 0.0074**
across models and variants. Variant A primary: specificity 0.534, PPV 0.00318,
NNE 315, **11.16 false-alert hours per patient-day**. SIRS at the same target
degenerates entirely (sensitivity 1.000, specificity 0.000, 23.96 FA-hours per
patient-day) — its apparent AUROC competitiveness is an artefact.

**Per-hour vs per-patient is where the numbers diverge by two orders of
magnitude**, which is exactly why the supervisor asked for both:

| Variant A, primary, 80% target | Per-hour | Per-patient |
|---|---|---|
| Sensitivity | 0.801 | 1.000 |
| PPV | 0.00318 | 0.0837 |
| Alert burden | 313.9 : 1 | — |
| False alarms / patient-day | 11.16 hours | 0.55 episodes |

Patient-level PPV (0.084) is **26×** the hourly PPV (0.0032). Reporting only one
of the two would materially mislead — hourly PPV alone reads as hopeless,
patient PPV alone reads as usable.

**Lead time.** Variant B primary: median **9 h** (IQR 4–22), 88.4% of detections
≥3 h before onset, 62.6% ≥6 h. Against the Moor et al. benchmark of 3.7 h, lead
time is *not* the failure mode — alert burden is.

### 3.4 Design rationale

Alarm episodes are counted as **contiguous runs of alert hours**, not by
clinician acknowledgement. A real deployment applies suppression, snoozing and
escalation, all of which reduce episode counts. So the thesis reports both and
states the bracket explicitly (Limitation 9): **alert-hour rates are the
conservative bound, episode rates the optimistic one, and the true burden lies
between them.** Reporting a single number would have required picking a side
with no evidence.

Operating points are derived on the same test set they are evaluated on, so
reported PPV and false-alarm rates are **optimistic** — a prospectively fixed
threshold would perform no better. Stated in Limitation 8 rather than left for a
reader to notice.

## 4. Hypotheses and multiple comparisons

**This is the change that altered the thesis's headline conclusion.**

### 4.1 Feedback

Be explicit about pre-specified vs post-hoc, account for multiple comparisons,
and put confidence intervals on all subgroup results. The equity tables reported
point-estimate AUROCs by subgroup with no CIs, standard errors, or bootstrap
intervals — a non-significant difference could have been written up as "no
disparity" when the interval might be enormous.

### 4.2 What changed — Protocol Amendment 1

A new terminal pipeline stage, **`12_inference.Rmd`**, produces three things:

1. **Stay-level cluster-bootstrap confidence intervals** (B = 2,000) for every
   headline and subgroup estimate.
2. **Multiplicity correction**: Holm-Bonferroni for the confirmatory family,
   Benjamini-Hochberg for each exploratory family. Bonferroni-adjusted p-values
   are reported alongside for reference.
3. **A machine-generated analysis register** (`12_analysis_register.csv` →
   `evaluation.tex` Table `tab:register`) tagging all 23 analyses.

Every result in `evaluation.tex` now carries an inline status tag — **[C]**
confirmatory, **[E]** exploratory, **[D]** descriptive — *in its own caption*,
not only in a general methodology statement.

**Family structure:**

| Family | Contents | m | Correction |
|---|---|---|---|
| Confirmatory | H1–H6 | 6 | Holm-Bonferroni, FWER 0.05 |
| E1 | Subgroup AUROC contrasts | 30 | Benjamini-Hochberg, q = 0.05 |
| E2 | Subgroup label-sensitivity contrasts | 18 | Benjamini-Hochberg, q = 0.05 |
| E3 | Alternative-label (D/E) contrasts | 4 | Benjamini-Hochberg, q = 0.05 |

φ_anchor sits **outside** every family: it is a ratio with no null at zero, so it
is reported as an interval only and never as a test.

### 4.3 Results — corrected confirmatory verdicts

| H | Contrast | Est. | 95% CI | p | p_Holm | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| H1 | label spread − model spread | 0.011 | −0.017 – 0.034 | 0.211 | 0.634 | **NOT confirmed** |
| H2 | unstable covariates | 18/20 | — | — | — | Descriptive (10 sign flips, 17 magnitude ≥2×) |
| H3 | pre-treatment onsets | **0** | — | — | — | Structural (0 in 179,003 person-h) |
| H4 | random − temporal AUROC | 0.123 | 0.100 – 0.147 | 5e-4 | 0.003 | **Confirmed** |
| H5 | internal − external AUROC | 0.170 | 0.102 – 0.242 | 1e-3 | 0.004 | **Confirmed** |
| H6 | GBT − primary AUROC | 0.135 | 0.113 – 0.156 | 5e-4 | 0.003 | **Rejected** (non-equivalence) |

**H1 flipped.** On point estimates alone the label spread (0.153) exceeds the
model spread (0.142) and H1 confirms, giving a tidy story in which the label
dominates. With intervals attached, the difference is 0.011 with a CI spanning
zero and the story is not supportable.

Variance decomposition (ΔAUROC): model class **0.142** [0.130–0.161] · label
**0.153** [0.136–0.177] · split **0.123** · metric **0.170**. **All four
intervals overlap — no factor ranks above another.**

The defensible claim is now narrower and more interesting: both label and model
contribute ~0.15 AUROC of variance, this study cannot separate them, and the
conventional literature — which fixes the label and reports model comparisons —
is measuring one source while ignoring another of **at least equal size**.
Detecting a 0.01 AUROC difference between two spreads would need a study
designed for it.

### 4.4 Results — subgroup analysis with intervals

Every subgroup AUROC now carries a cluster-bootstrap CI
(`12_subgroup_auroc_ci.csv`, figure `12_subgroup_auroc_ci.png`). The intervals
are what the feedback anticipated:

| Subgroup (primary, race) | n events | AUROC | 95% CI | CI width |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| WHITE (reference) | 597 | 0.614 | 0.590 – 0.637 | 0.047 |
| BLACK/AFRICAN AMERICAN | 58 | 0.627 | 0.542 – 0.706 | 0.164 |
| ASIAN | 16 | 0.628 | 0.455 – 0.788 | **0.334** |
| HISPANIC/LATINO – PUERTO RICAN | 16 | 0.570 | 0.411 – 0.735 | **0.324** |

An interval 0.33 wide cannot distinguish "no disparity" from "a disparity larger
than any effect reported anywhere else in this thesis."

**No race contrast survives BH correction** (smallest q = 0.310). The only E1
survivor is language-Haitian on GBT (Δ = +0.137, CI 0.024–0.231, q = 0.030,
n = 12 events) — flagged, not interpreted.

**The label-sensitivity contrasts (E2) do survive**, and are the better-powered
of the two equity analyses because they are stay-level proportions rather than
discrimination metrics, and do not depend on the small number of test-window
onsets: race-Unknown **+3.01 pp** (q = 0.006), Unable-to-obtain **+3.49 pp**
(q = 0.009), Spanish **+2.61 pp**, Private insurance **−1.76 pp**.

### 4.5 Design rationale

**Why the equity result is written as underpowered, not null.** Limitation 10
states it directly: *"This is a statement about precision, not about fairness:
the data are consistent both with no disparity and with disparities larger than
any effect this thesis reports elsewhere."* Detecting a subgroup AUROC gap of
the size seen in the point estimates would need roughly **ten times** the event
count available in the smaller groups. This is precisely the misreading the
feedback warned about, and it is now pre-empted in the text.

**Why H2 and H3 still consume family slots.** Neither admits a test statistic —
H2 is a stability criterion and the stored multinomial fit carries no variance
estimate; H3 is degenerate because its outcome is entirely absent from the
evaluation window. Both are reported descriptively but **retain their slot in
m = 6**, which makes the Holm correction on the remaining four hypotheses
*stricter*, not weaker. Dropping them to m = 4 would have been the
self-serving choice.

**Why the register is generated, not maintained by hand.** `tab:register` is
built from `12_analysis_register.parquet` by the pipeline, so it cannot drift
away from what the code actually computes. A hand-maintained table would be
stale within one re-run.

**Why the amendment is disclosed as post-hoc.** Limitation 12: intervals and
multiplicity control were added *after* the analyses had been run and inspected.
They change no estimand, threshold or direction — but a reader should treat the
corrected verdicts as a pre-specified hypothesis set analysed under a
retrospectively specified error-control plan, not as a fully pre-specified
confirmatory design. Given that the amendment *overturned* the study's most
attractive finding, the disclosure costs nothing and buys credibility.

`12_inference.Rmd` reads only Parquet outputs of earlier stages, so it re-runs
without refitting anything (~14 min). The bootstrap resamples **ICU stays, never
person-hours**, and evaluates AUROC/AUPRC as weighted rank statistics on
pre-sorted data so 2,000 replicates over half a million person-hours stay
tractable. For subgroup analyses the bootstrap is stratified within group.

US Common Rule **45 CFR 46.104(d)(4)** and PhysioNet's access policies.

**Single-centre stated as Limitation 1**, first in the list:

> All models were developed on MIMIC-IV, which originates from a single academic
> medical centre (Beth Israel Deaconess Medical Center, Boston). Clinical
> practices, patient demographics, and EHR documentation patterns at BIDMC may
> not represent those at other institutions. The equity and label-sensitivity
> findings in particular should not be assumed to transfer to hospitals with
> different patient populations or antibiotic prescribing cultures.

**Reproducibility documented** — `design.tex` §Reproducibility:

- **Seed 42 everywhere.** `set.seed(42)` in every Rmd setup chunk before any
  computation; the same value for `RANDOM_SPLIT_SEED` in `config.R`, the
  `pmsampsize` calculation, and all model-fitting calls. **No seed is drawn from
  wall-clock time or left to the system default.** Verified bit-for-bit
  reproducible on re-run.
- **Run script**: `run_pipeline.R` / `run_pipeline.sh` executes all stages
  sequentially or by phase, renders each stage to `output/pdf/`, and writes
  per-stage and combined logs to `output/logs/`. End-to-end 60 min on Apple
  silicon; stage 05 dominates (multinomial fit over 2.3 M person-hours per
  variant).
- **Environment override**: set `MIMIC_RESEARCH_ROOT` to the directory
  containing `data/`.
- **Data format**: all intermediate outputs Parquet; CSV only for small result
  tables (<1,000 rows) for readability.

**Code availability** — `design.tex` `sec:code_availability`. Repository is
version-controlled and **available from the author on request, including to
examiners**. It is currently private and contains **code only**: no MIMIC-IV or
eICU-CRD record, derived person-hour table, or fitted model object, because the
PhysioNet DUA prohibits redistribution. `.gitignore` verified — **0 patient-data
files tracked.**

The MIMIC-IV version (**v3.1**) and eICU-CRD version (**v2.0**) are stated
throughout.

### 5.4 Design rationale

The licence is deliberately recorded as **undetermined** rather than asserted.
An earlier draft claimed a "permissive open-source licence"; no `LICENSE` file
exists, so the code is currently all-rights-reserved and the claim was false.
The claim was removed on 2026-07-30. Apache-2.0 is the recommendation on the
table (patent grant, stronger warranty disclaimer for clinical-adjacent code,
NOTICE file for citation), with CC BY 4.0 for prose — **pending a check of ATU
student-IP policy**. Stating "not yet fixed" is accurate; stating a licence that
does not exist is not.

The repository URL is omitted from the thesis by the author's choice; the
Code Availability section says "available from the author on request" instead.

## 7. Where the thesis is still exposed

Stated here so it is not discovered at viva.

1. **The review is narrative, not systematic.** No screening pass over a
   de-duplicated yield, no second reviewer. C1 and C5 are hedged and bounded,
   but an examiner may still press on whether a systematic search would find a
   prior C5-equivalent study.
2. **Amendments 1 and 2 are both post-hoc.** Disclosed, never presented as
   pre-specified — but the confirmatory design is weaker than a fully
   pre-specified one, and Amendment 1 overturned H1.
3. **Variant D is unvalidated and partly self-fulfilling.** Its AUROC *level*
   must never be compared with B's. Only the κ / onset-shift disagreement and
   the event-count asymmetry carry weight. Say the caveat every time D is cited.
4. **The equity analysis is underpowered.** No race contrast survives BH; the
   sole E1 survivor rests on 12 events. Present as precision-limited, never as
   evidence of fairness.
5. **eICU external validation is weak** — 63 events over 7.87 M person-hours.
   eICU microbiology covers only ~1.5% of stays, so infection triggers on
   **antibiotics alone**. This is a **label transport failure, not a population
   difference**, and the caveat must accompany every external result.
6. **Licence undecided, repository private.** Blocks any "fully reproducible,
   openly available" framing. Needs an ATU student-IP policy check.
7. **SEP-1, CDC ASE and PhysioNet/CinC 2019 remain unimplemented.** Correctly
   rejected as treatment-independence tests, but a cohort-level comparison is
   real future work.
8. **No deep survival comparator**, and no notes / procedures / ventilator
   settings in the feature set.

## 8. Maintenance note

The thesis **hardcodes every number** and keeps its **own copy** of every figure
in `v2-thesis/images/`. Nothing auto-reads pipeline output. After any pipeline
re-run:

1. `cp v2-proj/output/figures/*.png v2-thesis/images/`
2. Re-check hardcoded tables against the ground-truth files.
3. `cd v2-thesis && ./build.sh`

**Use the GBT rows as the canary.** XGBoost is not bit-stable; on 2026-07-30 a
re-run refit GBT slightly differently, every non-GBT number still matched, and
every GBT number went stale — cascading into H1, H6, the decomposition, the
abstract and the conclusion. If the GBT rows match, the rest almost certainly
does. When adding a variant, always use `V2_VARIANTS=<ids>` to avoid an
incidental refit of A/B/C.

Ground-truth files for everything in this document are listed in
`v2-GENAI/HANDOFF.md` §5. Do not dump `output/processed_data/` — it is ~90 files.
