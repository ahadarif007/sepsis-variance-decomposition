### F1 (High). The two models' Utility Scores do not differ by "less than one hundredth"

| | |
|---|---|
| **Location** | `conclusion.tex` §H6 |
| **Stated** | "the two models are separated by less than one hundredth of a Utility Score point (0.039 against 0.025)" |
| **Actual** | `\pcUtilityMaxGbtB` = 0.039, `\pcUtilityMaxPrimaryB` = 0.025. The difference is **0.0142**, roughly one and a half hundredths |
| **Cause** | A qualitative comparison written beside two macros, so the macro system could not check it. Both values are correct; the sentence about them is not |
| **Correction** | Rewritten to state both scores and describe the gap as "little more than a hundredth of a point on a scale bounded by 1" |

The argument the sentence was making survives intact; only its arithmetic was
wrong. It is a clean example of the class round 11 named: prose adjacent to a
generated value is outside the generator's guarantee.

### F2 (High). Table 5.14's caption overstates the per-hour/per-patient alarm gap by a factor of two

| | |
|---|---|

## 2. The screening added this round

Round 11 specified a literal-number linter (§7.2) and it was never built. It
now exists as **`project/check_thesis_numbers.R`**, and it earned its place
immediately by finding F8.

**How it decides.** Value-matching against `pipeline_constants.tex` was tried
first and abandoned: with 1,223 generated quantities in scope, a
three-decimal figure from the literature coincides with one of them often
enough that the match carries no information. The linter therefore classifies
by **provenance**, not arithmetic. Every result-shaped literal (three or more
decimal places, or a comma-thousands integer) falls into one of three buckets:

| Bucket | Meaning | Count now |
|---|---|---|
| **CITED** | a citation appears in the same paragraph, so it is quoting prior work | 51 |
| **ALLOWED** | on an explicit list of design constants and source-data facts, each with its reason written beside it | 15 |
| **REVIEW** | unaccounted for: either it becomes a macro, or the list gains an entry | **0** |

Exit status is 1 while anything is in REVIEW, so the allow-list can only grow
by a deliberate act with a written reason. One- and two-decimal numbers are
excluded on purpose: in this document they are pre-registered thresholds, file
sizes or TikZ geometry, and a linter that fires on those is one people stop
reading, which is the failure mode that let the earlier defects through.

Two assertions were also added to **`check_consistency.R`**: no analysis name
appears twice in the register, and no two register rows report into the same
table. The second is what catches F7; the first cannot, because the duplicate
carries a different name.

### Claim predicates, and the limit of what can be mechanised

The deeper lesson of F1, F2, F4 and F5 is that a **value** can live in a macro
but a **claim about values** cannot. "More than tenfold", "less than one
hundredth", "the two smallest ratios" are relations, and they only become false
when the underlying numbers move in a particular direction. Nothing in the
generator can see that.

So `check_consistency.R` gained a section of **claim predicates**: sentences the
thesis states in words, written as assertions on the result tables. Eight now
exist, and each backs a specific sentence:

| Predicate | Sentence it defends |
|---|---|
| `sign + magnitude - overlap == unstable` | "which is why the two counts sum to seven across six covariates" |
| exactly one covariate meets both criteria | "vasopressor exposure meets both" |
| the two smallest noise ratios are the two sign reversals | "bilirubin and vasopressor exposure carry the two smallest ratios in the table" |
| no sign reversal exceeds its pooled SE | "both sign reversals among them, move by less than their own estimation noise" |
| model spread ≥ 10 × label spread | "more than tenfold"; "roughly an order of magnitude" |
| ceiling over A/B/C is GBT under C | the abstract's "or \pcUtilityMaxGbtC under any pre-registered variant" |
| ceiling under B is GBT under B | the abstract's "no model exceeds \pcUtilityMaxGbtB" |
| κ(A,B) < κ(C,B) | "the two narrowest readings agree at **only** κ" |

**Add one whenever a chapter asserts a relation rather than a value.** That is
the only mechanism by which a re-run can report that a sentence has gone false.

What deliberately stays a human read: `check_thesis_numbers.R` also prints an
**advisory** list of comparative claims carrying no macro in their sentence
(currently 25: "an order of magnitude", "twice as many", "tenfold"). It does not
gate on them. Gating would need an allow-list of every idiomatic use, and a
checker people silence by reflex is worse than no checker. Twenty-five lines is
scannable, and it is the right list to re-read after a run that moved numbers.

### A third checker: the margin gate

`check_thesis_margins.R` measures the built PDF, because LaTeX structurally
cannot. It infers the text block's right edge from the document (the *mode* of
per-page glyph maxima, since pages ending mid-paragraph drag an average left of
the true edge), exempts page 1 for the ATU title page's own `\newgeometry`, and
reports any page beyond it. Regression-tested against the pre-fix PDF, where it
correctly reports F10's 40 pt overrun and names the offending cells.

### All three are wired in

None of these needs remembering. `run_pipeline.sh` runs the first two after
`thesis_constants.R`; `build.sh` runs the third on the PDF it has just built.

```bash
cd sepsis/project
Rscript check_consistency.R       # cross-file arithmetic + claim predicates
Rscript check_thesis_numbers.R    # literal provenance over the thesis sources
Rscript check_thesis_margins.R    # does anything pass the text block
```

`run_pipeline.sh` exit codes: **1** a stage failed · **2** an unresolved macro or
table body · **3** a consistency check failed · **4** an unprovenanced literal.
`build.sh` exits non-zero on a page that overruns.

## 6. Verdict

**Defensible with minor revisions**, and after the run in §5, submission-ready.

The evidence for "defensible": four non-confirmations reported as findings
rather than repaired around; seven amendments disclosed as post-hoc, with the
two that could be read as researcher degrees of freedom each carrying its own
written defence; a label critique supported by measurement rather than
argument; external validation whose limits are stated more honestly than the
literature it cites; and a generated-constant system that, on this round's
evidence, does hold, since every defect found lives outside it rather than in
it.

The evidence for "minor" rather than "major": twelve defects, none touching a
result, an interval or a verdict, and three of them single wrong numbers in
otherwise correct sentences. That is a different document from the one round 11
examined, which shipped a PDF containing thirteen visible placeholders.

The pattern worth naming, because it is the same one round 11 named and it has
now produced its next instance: **every defect this round found sits in the gap
between a generated value and a sentence written next to it.** F1 is a
comparison of two macros, F2 a ratio of two columns, F5 a sum of two counts,
F3 a consequence of a table not carried into the chapters that cite it. The
macro system cannot reach any of these. `check_thesis_numbers.R` closes the
narrowest part of that gap mechanically; the rest is still a read, and F3 is
the argument for doing that read against the *evidence tables* rather than
against the previous draft.
