## 1. The four findings that changed a claim

Ordered by examination risk, not by effort.

### 1.1 H6 was reported as two-sided equivalence and is not

**The thesis said:** *"The whole interval lies inside the equivalence bound
θ₀ = 0.02"*, and, in the conclusion, *"lies entirely inside the pre-registered
0.02 equivalence margin"*.

**The interval is [−0.0272, +0.0019] and the margin is ±0.02.** The lower limit
is outside it. The claim is arithmetically false as written, in the section the
thesis calls *the strongest positive result in the study*, which is where an
examiner reads most carefully.

The underlying error is inferential rather than typographical. `12_inference.Rmd`
computes H6's p-value as

```r
p_h6 <- boot_pvalue(boot_h1[, "h6_delta"], obs_h1h6["h6_delta"], 0.02, "greater")
```

— one-sided, against the upper bound only. Two-sided equivalence needs the
second of the two one-sided tests as well, and the data do not deliver it. A
further step was being taken silently: failing to reject a null of
non-equivalence was being read as evidence *for* equivalence, which is
accepting the null.

**What is actually established** is non-inferiority in the direction that
matters: the boosted trees are not better than the interpretable model by more
than the margin. What is *not* established is that the two are interchangeable
— a difference of up to ~0.027 in the interpretable model's favour remains
consistent with these data.

Rewritten in **seven** places, not the four the audit first listed:
`evaluation.tex` (H6 box, body, the decomposition bullet, the pattern
paragraph), `conclusion.tex`, `discussion.tex` (×3), `README.md`. A paragraph
now states the asymmetry explicitly and notes that the conclusion only ever
needed one side of it: the argument for preferring an opaque model *is* the
argument that it performs better, and that is excluded.

**This weakens nothing that the thesis concludes.** It removes a claim the
thesis did not need and could not defend.

### 1.2 H5 is tested on a substituted estimand, and the substitution was undeclared

§7 states H5 as a comparison of **proportional declines** — Utility Score
falling proportionally more than AUROC from internal to external validation.
What is tested, and what carries H5's Holm-adjusted p-value, is
**internal − external AUROC against θ₀ = 0**. Those are different estimands.

The substitution is defensible: a proportional decline needs a baseline, and
the internal Utility Score is 0.000 at the default cut-off and 0.025 at its
best threshold. But Amendment 1 §13.6 lists only H2 and H3 as degenerate, and
H5 was not among them. So the protocol declared two departures and made three.

Aggravating, and the reason this ranked second: **the substituted estimand
produces the family's smallest raw p-value (0.092), the only one below 0.10.**
An examiner finding an undeclared estimand swap that happens to yield the
study's best result is entitled to ask how it came about.

Now recorded in §13.6 with three binding constraints — the substitution is not
an alternative route to the pre-registered claim; its direction and threshold
are the ones §4 already fixed, so no new degree of freedom is taken; and the
Utility limb is reported descriptively and internally only. Flagged in the H5
results box and in the limitations, both stating that the adjusted p-value
belongs to the substitute.

### 1.3 The prediction framing described a model that was not fitted

Methodology stated a **6-hour prediction horizon** — *"will sepsis onset occur
within the next 6 hours?"*. The models estimate the **current-hour
cause-specific hazard**, and every AUROC, AUPRC and calibration figure in the
thesis scores that hazard against an indicator marking the onset hour among the
at-risk hours. The 6-hour window enters only through the Utility Score's
lead-time reward, episode sensitivity and the lead-time distribution.

This is the finding with the widest blast radius, because it governs how every
discrimination number in Chapter 5 should be read, and it makes the reported
AUROCs **not comparable** with published figures from studies classifying a
binary "onset within *k* hours" label — those have *k* times more positives and
an easier scoring task. Comparisons of that kind appear in Chapter 2.

Rewritten as an explicit estimand, with a "what is estimated versus what is
scored" paragraph, and a new limitation covering all three consequences: the
non-comparability, the fact that covariates entering the onset-hour prediction
are contemporaneous with it (so this measures detection at the recorded onset
rather than anticipation of it), and the absence of a cumulative six-hour risk.

### 1.4 The feature list described a model that was not fitted, twice over

Methodology listed **P/F ratio** among the laboratory value predictors and
**lactate** among the rolling trends, and omitted the **heart-rate slope**.
`BASE_FEATURES` contains `hr_slope6h`, no lactate slope, and `pf_ratio` only as
`pf_ratio_measured` — an order indicator, not a value.

The predictor count was also unreconciled across the document: 25 in the
sample-size section, 29 elsewhere, 22 in the code. All three were describing
different objects and none said which.

Corrected, with per-category counts, and both boundaries now explained rather
than left to look like errors — why the P/F ratio contributes only its order
flag, and why there is no lactate slope. Reconciled to **22 covariates / 29
terms per outcome**, the second confirmed independently by the run: stage 05
now logs *"Cluster-robust SEs computed for 87 parameters"*, and 87 = 29 × 3
non-reference outcomes.

## 2. The pre-registration had stopped being current

Three amendments were in force in the thesis, the code, or both, and absent
from `00_preregistration.md`.

| Amendment | State before this round |
|---|---|
| **5** Action-derived ablation | In the thesis and in `config.R`; **not** in the pre-registration. `methodology.tex` cited it as "§16 of `00_preregistration.md`", which is Amendment 2 |
| **6** Subgroup interpretability floor | Nowhere. Family E1 fell from 30 contrasts to 6 with no protocol record at all |
| **7** H2 uncertainty diagnostic | Created this round — see §5 |

Amendment 6 is the one that mattered. §14.2 of the inference plan says the
correction denominator is "the number of contrasts **actually computed**, not
the number reported", and that "selecting a subset for presentation does not
shrink the family". Reducing E1 from 30 to 6 is on its face the manoeuvre that
rule prohibits, and it was undocumented — so an examiner would have found an
unexplained family reduction that removed a significant finding, with the
protocol's own rule against it sitting two sections away.

§20.4 now states the tension in those words and offers four reasons the floor
is admissible, inviting the reader to reject them:

1. The rule's target is a different manoeuvre — below-floor contrasts are still
   estimated and still shown; they are removed from the *inferential* family.
2. The criterion is outcome-blind: stratum event count alone, and the constant
   is shared with the external analysis, where it was fixed before the eICU
   event counts were known.
3. **The direction is exculpatory.** The reduction removed the family's only
   surviving discovery — a language contrast on 12 events — rather than
   creating one. A family narrowed to manufacture significance narrows *around*
   its significant results.
4. The verdict is invariant: nothing in E1 survives BH under either
   denominator. At m = 6 the smallest adjusted value is q = 0.066.

Amendment count updated to **1–7** in `appendix.tex`, `README.md`,
`declaration.tex`, `methodology.tex` and `discussion.tex`.

## 5. Amendment 7 — the H2 uncertainty diagnostic

This is the one finding that produced new analysis rather than a correction.

**How it arose.** Round 8 (`v3.md` §5b) implemented `estfun.multinom` so that
`sandwich` would work on the primary model, but the code had never been run.
This round ran it: all nine fits produced a cluster-robust covariance, zero
failures, 87 parameters, median SE 0.0105. **And then nothing consumed it** —
no `se_cluster` column, no stage reading the `.rds`, no standard error anywhere
in the thesis. The document would have said a cluster-robust covariance is
computed and shown none, which invites exactly one follow-up: *then does H2's
instability exceed its standard error?*

**What the answer turned out to be.** H2 flags a covariate when its point
estimate reverses sign or doubles in magnitude across variants A, B and C. That
is a threshold rule on point estimates, and it cannot separate a coefficient
that genuinely moves from one estimated too imprecisely for a doubling to mean
anything. Referring each flagged covariate's extreme difference to the pooled
standard error of the two estimates involved:

| Covariate | β_A | β_B | β_C | max diff | pooled SE | ratio | kind |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `wbc_measured` | 0.0969 | 0.5374 | 0.8989 | 0.8020 | 0.0517 | **15.51** | action |
| `platelets_measured` | −0.2563 | −0.5614 | −0.8173 | 0.5610 | 0.0529 | **10.60** | action |
| `creatinine_measured` | 0.2877 | 0.2303 | 0.0343 | 0.2534 | 0.1024 | **2.48** | action |
| `platelets` | 0.0002 | 0.0001 | 0.0003 | 0.0002 | 0.0002 | 1.07 | physiol. |
| `vaso_any` | −0.0374 | 0.0271 | 0.0025 | 0.0645 | 0.0864 | 0.75 | action |
| `bilirubin_total` | 0.0014 | −0.0016 | −0.0020 | 0.0034 | 0.0056 | 0.61 | physiol. |

**Three of the six move by less than their own estimation noise, and all three
that survive are order indicators.** The separation is clean rather than
marginal — nothing falls between 1.07 and 2.48 — so the split does not depend
on where the cut is placed, which is stated in the caption because "3 of 6"
would otherwise invite the question.

**The decision on how to report it.** H2's headline **stays 6 of 22**.
Restating it as 3 would substitute a post-hoc estimand for a pre-registered
one, which is precisely the practice this thesis objects to elsewhere — and the
substitution would be *flattering*, which is a reason for more caution rather
than less. §21.3 makes the ordering binding: pre-registered count first and
unmodified, diagnostic second and separately labelled.

**Two constraints written in against the result**, because it favours the
thesis's own argument:

- **It is not a test.** The three fits share stays, so the differenced
  estimates are correlated and a pooled SE assuming independence is
  conservative by an unquantified amount; and because the specification is
  ridge-penalised out of necessity (Amendment 3), the sandwich is
  ridge-regularised. No p-value is computed and §21.2 says why.
- **It is not independent corroboration of Amendment 5.** The ablation's
  by-kind split, the ablation arm and this diagnostic all come from the same
  three fitted models and share the same flagged set. Three readings of one
  body of evidence, not three experiments agreeing. §21.4 records this *in
  advance* of interpreting the result, and the thesis repeats it in the results
  section and the limitations.

**Built:** `attach_cluster_se()` and `h2_stability_uncertainty()` in `utils.R`;
`se_cluster` on the stage-05 coefficient tables, joined on `"<outcome>:<term>"`
and never by position; `12_h2_stability_uncertainty` from stage 12; 8 macros
and `table_htwouncertainty` generated whole; new `sec:h2_uncertainty` in
Results, a methodology paragraph, a register row, and a reconciled limitation.
