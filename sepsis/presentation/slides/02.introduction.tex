\begin{frame}{I. Introduction: Discrimination and Clinical Utility}
\begin{itemize}\itemsep2pt\small
  \item Sepsis is associated with about \textbf{11 million deaths a year},
        nearly one fifth of deaths worldwide~\cite{rudd2020global}. Each
        additional hour before antibiotics is associated with higher
        mortality~\cite{seymour2017time}.
  \item Wang et al.~\cite{wang2025srpm} reviewed \textbf{91} real-time sepsis
        prediction models:
\end{itemize}

\begin{center}\tablestyle\footnotesize
\begin{tabular}{lcc}
\toprule
\textbf{Metric} & \textbf{Internal} & \textbf{External} \\
\midrule
Median AUROC         & 0.811            & 0.783 \\
Median utility score & \textbf{$+$0.381} & \textbf{$-$0.164} \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\begin{itemize}\itemsep2pt\small
  \item Negative utility indicates performance below the no-alert strategy.
        AUROC measures ranking, not alert timing or clinical value, and
        therefore did not reflect the decline in utility.
  \item Deployment: Epic Sepsis Model v2, 227,091 encounters, AUROC
        0.82--0.92, PPV 0.13--0.26~\cite{wong2026epic}.
\end{itemize}

\vfill
\begin{alertblock}{}
  \centering\small Model comparisons alone do not establish robustness or
  clinical utility.
\end{alertblock}
\end{frame}
