\begin{frame}{II. Background: Limitations of the Conventional Approach}
\vspace*{0pt}%
{\centering Choose one Sepsis-3 definition $\rightarrow$ use one train/test
split $\rightarrow$ report AUROC $\rightarrow$ compare models.\par}

\vspace{2pt}
\begin{enumerate}\itemsep1pt\small
  \item \textbf{The sepsis label is not fixed.} Different ways of applying
        Sepsis-3 to the same database gave 867--2,178
        cases~\cite{cohen2024subtle}.
  \item \textbf{The data split can inflate results.} Sepsis prediction suffers
        the largest drop of any clinical task under a temporal
        shift~\cite{guo2022temporal}.
  \item \textbf{AUROC hides the real failure.} A model can keep a high AUROC
        while its clinical usefulness becomes harmful~\cite{wang2025srpm}.
  \item \textbf{The label captures doctor behaviour.} Models may learn when
        doctors suspect sepsis and treat, not when the patient
        worsens~\cite{kamran2024evaluation}.
\end{enumerate}

\vspace{2pt}
\begin{block}{This thesis}
  \small Instead of fixing the label, the split and the metric and asking which model
  wins, it treats all of them as \textbf{sources of variance} and estimates
  each contribution \textbf{with an interval attached}, so that the ordering
  between them is established rather than assumed.
\end{block}
\end{frame}
