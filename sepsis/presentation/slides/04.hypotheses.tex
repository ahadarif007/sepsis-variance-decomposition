\begin{frame}{III. Research Question and Pre-Registered Hypotheses}
\begin{block}{Research Question}
  \small In real-time sepsis prediction on MIMIC-IV~\cite{johnson2023mimiciv},
  how much of the reported performance variation comes from (a)~the choice of
  model, and (b)~the researcher's decisions: how sepsis is defined, how data
  is split, when prediction is made, and how performance is measured?
\end{block}

\vspace{2pt}
\begin{itemize}\itemsep0pt\footnotesize
  \item \textbf{H1 (Label dominance):} changing the sepsis definition affects
        AUROC at least as much as changing the model.
  \item \textbf{H2 (Coefficient instability):} at least one feature changes
        direction, or magnitude $\geq 2\times$, across label variants.
  \item \textbf{H3 (Treatment leakage):} restricting evaluation to before
        treatment reduces AUROC by $\geq 0.03$.
  \item \textbf{H4 (Split optimism):} temporal testing gives $\geq 0.02$ lower
        AUROC than random testing.
  \item \textbf{H5 (Metric divergence):} Utility declines more than AUROC from
        internal to external validation.
  \item \textbf{H6 (Model-class null):} the primary statistical model is not
        outperformed by gradient-boosted trees by more than 0.02 AUROC.
\end{itemize}

\vspace{2pt}
{\footnotesize One confirmatory family, Holm--Bonferroni ($m = 6$).
\textbf{Scope:} the models estimate the hazard of onset in the
\emph{current} hour, i.e.\ detection at the recorded onset, not advance
warning.\par}
\end{frame}
